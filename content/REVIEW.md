### This, that, and plurals (`i-4`)
Sources consulted: Wiktionary, "yii" (Yoruba section): yìí, this, placed after the noun (Ọmọ yìí ni tèmi, this child is mine); a short form of èyí.; Wiktionary, "yẹn" (Yoruba section): yẹn, that, placed after the noun (Mo fẹ́ ka ìwé yẹn, I want to read that book); a short form of ìyẹn. The low-tone yẹ̀n is an Ìjẹ̀bú and Ìkálẹ̀ form.; Wiktionary, "wọnyi" and "wọnyẹn" (Yoruba sections): wọ̀nyí, these; wọ̀nyẹn, those.; Wiktionary, "awọn" (Yoruba section): àwọn, a particle that comes before a noun to mark it as plural; àwọ̀n (low, low) is a different word, net.; COERLL, Yorùbá Yé Mi (University of Texas at Austin), Chapter 2, Lesson 2 "The Plural marker àwọn", page 55: àwọn goes before the noun (Àwọn ilé, houses; Àwọn ọmọdé, children), is sometimes left out when the plural is clear, and can mark respect for one person.
