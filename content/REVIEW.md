### This, that, and plurals (`i-4`)
Sources consulted: Wiktionary, "yii" (Yoruba section): yìí, this, placed after the noun (Ọmọ yìí ni tèmi, this child is mine); a short form of èyí.; Wiktionary, "yẹn" (Yoruba section): yẹn, that, placed after the noun (Mo fẹ́ ka ìwé yẹn, I want to read that book); a short form of ìyẹn. ### Vowel elision and contraction (`a-1`)
Sources consulted: COERLL, Yorùbá Yé Mi (University of Texas at Austin), Chapter 10, Lesson 2 "Vowel Assimilation, Vowel Lengthening, and Vowel Deletion", page 243: kú + ilé = kúulé, kú + ìrọ̀lẹ́ = kúùrọ̀lẹ́, ojú + irin = ojúurin; assimilation can go either way (kú + alẹ́ = káalẹ́). Page 245: contraction drops one of two vowels that meet across a word boundary: rí ẹja = rẹ́ja (to see fish), ta iṣu = taṣu (to sell yams), ra ẹran = rẹran (to buy meat), ti ọ̀rẹ́ = tọ̀rẹ́. COERLL says assimilation and contraction are two different processes. The PDF uses a legacy font encoding for tone marks.; Wikipedia, "Yoruba language", section "Assimilation and elision" (citing Bamgboṣe 1965 and Ward 1952): ra ẹja becomes rẹja (buy fish); elision is absent only in slow, unnatural speech; the spelling follows speech and writes the contracted form as one word; some writers mark a dropped vowel with an apostrophe (n'ílé).; Wiktionary, "ta", "ra", "ẹran", "iṣu" (Yoruba sections): tà, to sell, said ta before a direct object; rà, to buy; ẹran, meat; iṣu, yam.; The practice sentences Mo rẹran, N kò rẹran and Ó taṣu are built from the contractions above and were not found written out in a source.; No source consulted states a general rule for which of the two vowels drops; this lesson teaches each pair as the sources give it. Not cross-checked against a print grammar.

### Relative clauses with tí (`a-4`)
Sources consulted: Lawal, "Yoruba relativisation and the continuous segment principle", Studies in African Linguistics 18(1), 1987, pages 68 to 72: tí follows the noun; when the noun is the subject of the clause, a pronoun (ó) stays in the clause, as in ọkùnrin tí ó ra aṣọ (the man who bought the cloth); when the noun is the object, nothing fills its place, as in aṣọ tí ọkùnrin náà rà (the cloth that the man bought).
